The math first

$2,000/week is roughly $104,000/year. That is a 20% annual return on $520,000 — or a 200%+ annual return on $50,000. For context: the S&P/TSX Composite has returned roughly 7–9%/year over the long run, most professional fund managers fail to beat it consistently, and large academic studies of retail traders repeatedly find the majority lose money after costs.

None of that makes the goal impossible — it makes it a capital + edge + years problem rather than a quick-setup problem. Use the calculator below to see exactly what your target implies, then work the phases in the Playbook.

Your assumptions

A 40% win rate with 2R average winners is a competent, developing trader. Most beginners run negative expectancy after costs — set these to what your own journal proves, not what you hope.

Read this first

Large academic studies of retail traders — Taiwanese day-trading records, Brazilian futures traders, multiple brokerage datasets — consistently find that the large majority of active traders lose money after costs over multi-year periods, and that the share who earn a consistent professional income is a fraction of one percent. The people who make it treat it as a skilled trade with a multi-year apprenticeship, not an income hack.

So reframe the goal: first prove positive expectancy over 100+ trades, then scale capital. Income is the output of edge × capital × time. The Reality Check tab shows the honest arithmetic; the phases below are the path.

The staged roadmap (edge first, dollars later)

  1. Phase 0 — Foundations (4–8 weeks). Pick ONE setup (e.g. pullback-in-uptrend or breakout) and write rules for it: what qualifies, where the stop goes, where the target is, when you're wrong. Build a nightly scan routine. Read: Trading in the Zone (Douglas), Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard (Minervini), The Daily Trading Coach (Steenbarger).
  2. Phase 1 — Paper trade (8–12 weeks). Trade the setup in a simulator or on paper exactly as written, logged in the Journal tab. Gate to pass: ≥ 30 trades, positive expectancy after realistic costs, complete journal.
  3. Phase 2 — Small real money (3–6 months). Real orders, 0.25–0.5% risk per trade. Real fills, real slippage, real emotions. Gate: ≥ 50 trades, expectancy ≥ +0.2R, max drawdown < 10%, rules followed on ≥ 90% of trades.
  4. Phase 3 — Scale (ongoing). Raise risk to ~1%, add a second setup, add capital only as the stats hold. Reassess quarterly against the Reality Check numbers. This is the phase where the weekly-income question becomes real — not before.

Your first 30 days (start tonight — none of this needs money at risk)

  • Week 1 — Plumbing. Open or dust off a self-directed account at a broker with bracket/OCO and GTC stop orders (see the table below) and find its paper-trading or demo mode. Read the first half of Trading in the Zone. Log one fake trade in the Journal tab and export the CSV so you know backups work.
  • Week 2 — One setup, in writing. Pick a single setup (e.g. pullback-in-uptrend) and write its rules: what qualifies, the entry trigger, where the stop goes, where the target is, what invalidates it. Build a watchlist of ~20 TSX names passing the $1M daily dollar-volume filter. No trading yet.
  • Week 3 — Paper trading begins. Run the evening routine below for real: scan, plan, size with the Position Sizer, "place" the paper orders. Log every trade — and every setup you skipped, with why. The checklist tab is mandatory.
  • Week 4 — First stats review. Read your journal's win rate, average R and expectancy. Whatever they say, the answer is "keep paper trading" — the Phase 1 gate is 30+ trades, and 30 days can't produce that. The habit is the win.

Nothing in month one requires risking a dollar, quitting anything, or being right about the market. It requires about 45 minutes on weeknights — which is also exactly what the real thing will require.

Trading around a full-time job

Swing trading (holding days to weeks) is the only style compatible with a 9–5 — day trading needs the open, and the open is when you're at work. The job is an advantage: it pays the bills so you never need to force a trade.

  • Evening routine (30–45 min, after 4pm ET close): review open positions → run your scan → shortlist setups → size them (Position Sizer tab) → queue the orders: a limit entry with an attached stop-loss and target (bracket / OCO orders — pick a broker that supports them, and use GTC for stops).
  • Morning (5 min before 9:30 ET): check for overnight news on your names; confirm orders are live. No impulse market orders at the open — your limits either fill or they don't.
  • During work: nothing. Price alerts on your phone do the watching. If a position needs babysitting, it was sized wrong or stopped wrong.
  • Weekend (1 hr): journal review, update stats, rebuild the watchlist, check market regime (is the TSX above its 200-day average? Is breadth healthy?). Trade smaller — or not at all — in hostile regimes.

Canadian market mechanics

  • Exchanges: TSX (large caps), TSX Venture (speculative small caps), CSE, Cboe Canada. Hours 9:30–16:00 ET. Stick to TSX names early — much of the TSXV/CSE is too illiquid to swing trade safely.
  • Liquidity filter: the TSX is thinner than US markets. Require ≥ $1M average daily dollar-volume (price × volume) so your exit doesn't move the market. That still leaves 200+ tradeable names.
  • Concentration: the index is heavy in financials, energy and materials — your "diversified" three positions can be one oil bet in disguise. Cap correlated exposure.
  • No US-style PDT rule: the American $25k pattern-day-trader minimum is a FINRA rule and doesn't apply at Canadian brokers (normal margin rules still do). Swing trading sidesteps it anyway.
  • Interlisted names & FX: many large TSX companies also trade in New York in USD. If you trade US-listed stock, watch currency conversion costs (~1.5% each way at some brokers) — journalling in CAD keeps your stats honest. "Norbert's gambit" is the classic cheap CAD↔USD conversion technique worth reading about.

Accounts & taxes — general info, not advice

Rules change and facts below are general, as of late 2025 / early 2026. Verify with the CRA and a licensed accountant before relying on any of it.

  • TFSA — careful. Gains are tax-free for investing, but the CRA can treat frequent, short-hold, business-like trading as running a business inside the TFSA and tax the profits as income — courts have upheld this against active traders. Occasional multi-week swings are a very different picture than constant turnover, but if your plan is high-frequency swing trading, get professional advice before doing it in a TFSA.
  • RRSP: tax-deferred growth and the business-income issue effectively doesn't arise the same way, but losses are trapped (no claiming them) and withdrawals are taxed income. US-listed dividends are treaty-exempt in an RRSP but face 15% withholding in a TFSA.
  • Non-registered (cash/margin): the default for an active trading experiment. Capital gains are currently taxed on a 50% inclusion basis (a proposed increase was shelved in 2025 — verify the current rate). If the CRA deems your activity a business (frequency, short holds, leverage, time spent), gains become fully-taxed business income — and losses fully deductible.
  • Superficial loss rule — swing traders trip on this: sell at a loss and re-buy the same security within 30 days (before or after, you or affiliated accounts) and the loss is denied for tax (it's added to the new position's cost base instead). Re-entering a favourite name two weeks later is exactly this.
  • Record-keeping: your broker's T5008 slips need checking against your own adjusted-cost-base records, in CAD, including USD trades converted at the transaction-date rate. The journal's CSV export gives you the raw data.

Risk rules that keep you alive

  • 1% rule: never risk more than 1% of the account between entry and stop (0.25–0.5% while proving the edge).
  • Max 3–5 open positions. More is unmanageable around a job, and correlated positions count as one.
  • Weekly circuit breaker: down 3R in a week → flat, no new trades until Monday. Losing streaks are when accounts die.
  • Never average down. Never move a stop away from price. The stop is the definition of "I was wrong."
  • No earnings holds until 100+ journalled trades prove you have an edge that survives binary events.
  • Journal every trade — the Journal tab computes the only numbers that matter: expectancy, profit factor, drawdown.
